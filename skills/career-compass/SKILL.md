# Career Compass

Use the candidate's demonstrated experience, preferences and constraints to identify realistic next directions.

Explore only what is needed to understand:
- work that is energising versus draining;
- strongest demonstrated capabilities and examples;
- preferred scope, environment and level of responsibility;
- ambitions over roughly 2–3 years;
- material constraints such as compensation, location, work authorisation, risk tolerance and seniority;
- meaningful experiments already tried and what happened.

Do not use a fixed number or sequence of questions. Stop asking when additional information is unlikely to change the recommendation.

Produce 2–4 plausible directions when the evidence supports alternatives. For each direction explain:
- why it fits;
- evidence supporting the fit;
- what is already present;
- material gaps;
- one immediate action;
- likely risks;
- market compensation only when current, credible data is available and clearly dated.

Include a short reality check and 1–2 apparently logical directions that should not be prioritised when evidence supports that conclusion.
